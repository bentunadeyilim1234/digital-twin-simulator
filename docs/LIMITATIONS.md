# Limitations

## Evidence and parameters

- **All default coefficients are illustrative.** None is an empirical effect size; the
  configuration carries `status: ILLUSTRATIVE` and the UI says so on every page.
- 30 of 38 behavioural relationships are research hypotheses (class H); the 8 E2
  relationships rest on associations between related constructs, mostly cross-sectional.
- The validated horizon is **0 months**: nothing has been validated against real school data.
- Several parameters are weakly identifiable from aggregate monthly data (e.g. H1 vs. H2 both
  act on trust; H4 needs substantial variation in trust). Calibration reports this; it cannot
  remove it.

## Structure

- Monthly time step: faster dynamics (weekly workload peaks, crises) are not represented.
- Linear target equations with clipping approximate saturation; true responses may be
  nonlinear or threshold-like.
- Context indicators respond to the state only through explicitly hypothesised edges; all
  other context paths are exogenous.
- Responsive context deviations are anchored to each run's initial state, so long-run
  equilibria depend on the initial conditions (documented, verified behaviour).
- One school is modelled as one unit; within-school heterogeneity (grades, groups, sites) is
  invisible, and readiness indices are weighted means that can hide within-domain variation
  (MIN_DOMAIN partly mitigates this).
- Support demand I(t) is generic aggregate demand; differences in complexity between processes
  are represented only through average hours per process.
- Interventions have constant effect sizes once ramped up; decay, fidelity and implementation
  quality are not modelled beyond the `end_month` option.
- Resource requirements are illustrative staff-hour totals, not budgets.

## Data

- Synthetic datasets are generated by the model itself and therefore cannot validate it.
- Survey aggregates inherit the validity and reliability of the instruments used; small samples
  are suppressed but measurement error remains.
- Observational data cannot establish causal direction; intervention validation via interrupted
  time series is fragile on short series.

## Statistical methods

- Least-squares standard errors are local Gauss–Newton approximations and unreliable near bounds
  or under autocorrelated errors.
- The Bayesian layer uses a gradient-free adaptive Metropolis sampler with a shared Gaussian error
  scale; with weakly identified parameters chains may mix slowly (reported via R̂/ESS). PyMC is an
  optional backend that is **not enabled** in this build.
- Sobol indices from small base samples have wide confidence intervals; Morris screening is a
  ranking tool, not a variance decomposition.

## Visualisation

- The 3D school is fictional and conceptual. Zones show school-level indicators associated with a
  function, not per-room measurements, and tints are plain means of those indicators.
- No security features, access points or movement paths are represented — by design.

## Software and deployment

- Workspace separation in hosted mode is not authentication (see `DATA_GOVERNANCE.md`).
- SQLite suits single-instance deployments; horizontal scaling would require a server database.
- The Dockerfiles and the Render blueprint were written but could not be built in the development
  environment (no container daemon available); the local and e2e workflows were tested.
